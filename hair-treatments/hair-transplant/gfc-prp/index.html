What is GFC PRP treatment?

GFC stands for Growth Factor Concentrate. It is a non-surgical, blood-derived hair restoration procedure in which platelet-related growth factors are separated, concentrated, and applied to thinning areas of the scalp.

GFC is often described as a modified form of PRP treatment. Early clinical research suggests that both PRP and GFC may help improve hair density in selected patients with pattern hair loss, although individual responses and treatment protocols vary. Key features of GFC PRP treatment include:

  • It is prepared using a small amount of the patient’s own blood.
  • The preparation process focuses on collecting concentrated growth factors.
  • It is a non-surgical treatment without graft extraction or implantation.
  • GFC may support weak or miniaturized hair follicles.
  • It may help reduce excessive shedding in selected patients.
  • Both men and women with patterned hair thinning may be considered.
  • It generally requires less recovery time than surgical hair restoration.
  • It may be used before or after a hair transplant when medically recommended.
  • Treatment sessions are planned according to the patient’s scalp condition.
  • Results develop gradually and cannot be guaranteed for every patient.

FAQ about GFC PRP treatment

Both treatments use components obtained from the patient’s blood. GFC focuses on a concentrated growth-factor preparation, while conventional PRP contains platelet-rich plasma. The better option depends on the patient’s diagnosis, scalp condition and recommended protocol.

GFC may support weakened follicles, reduce shedding and improve hair density in suitable cases. It is not likely to regrow hair in completely bald areas without active follicles.

Many patients require a planned course of multiple sessions. The exact number and interval should be determined after scalp assessment rather than following the same schedule for everyone.

Some patients may first notice reduced shedding, followed by gradual improvement in hair quality or density. Visible changes may take several months because hair grows in natural cycles.

Mild pinching, pressure or temporary scalp sensitivity may occur during injection. A suitable numbing method can be used to improve comfort when clinically appropriate.

Temporary redness, tenderness, swelling, itching or sensitivity may occur. Because the preparation comes from the patient’s own blood, allergic reactions may be less likely, but proper medical assessment and sterile application remain essential.
