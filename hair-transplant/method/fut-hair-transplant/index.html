What is FUT hair transplant?

FUT, also called the strip method, is one of the two main techniques in hair restoration, alongside FUE. During the procedure, the surgeon removes a narrow strip of skin from the permanent donor zone at the back and sides of the head. A team then separates the strip into natural groups of one to four hairs, called follicular units. These units are placed into tiny channels in the thinning or bald area, and the donor wound is closed with fine sutures.

Because the grafts are cut out as intact units and handled under magnification, FUT can supply many grafts in one session while keeping them well protected. The trade-off is a thin linear scar at the donor site, which we explain openly below.

FUT hair transplant services at HairTreat

FUT can be used in several areas of the scalp. After your examination, the doctor tells you which areas are worth treating first and how many grafts each one needs.

Hairline restoration

A receding or uneven front line is rebuilt with fine single-hair grafts for a soft edge that suits your forehead and facial proportions. If an earlier result left you unhappy, see our hairline correction service.

Frontal and mid-scalp coverage

For thinning across the top of the head, grafts are distributed from the hairline back through the mid-scalp. FUT is often considered here because it can supply many grafts in a single session.

Crown and vertex restoration

Crown hair grows in a swirl, so grafts are placed to follow that natural direction. The result looks full from every angle instead of flat or stiff.

Advanced baldness (Norwood 5 to 7)

Large bald areas need the most grafts and the most careful planning. FUT can be useful here when the donor strip is suitable. The doctor explains what can safely be covered in one session and whether a second session would give a better outcome.

Combined FUT and FUE planning

In some cases, FUT is used for the main graft requirement, and FUE is kept in reserve for later touch-ups or fine hairline work. This approach can help you make the most of your donor supply over the years.

Key points about FUT hair transplant

A good FUT result depends on planning the donor strip, graft count, and hairline carefully, so these are the points to understand before you decide.

  • Your own hair: Grafts come from your donor area, so the transplanted hair grows like natural hair for the long term.
  • Large sessions: FUT can provide a high number of grafts in one sitting when the scalp is flexible enough for a strip.
  • Undamaged follicles: Because grafts are separated by hand under magnification, the follicles are well protected.
  • Donor hair stays long: The donor area is not shaved over a wide zone, which many patients prefer.
  • A linear scar: FUT leaves a thin line at the donor site. Hair of a normal length usually covers it, but a very short haircut can reveal it.
  • Stitches and a little more downtime: The donor wound needs time to heal, and the sutures are removed after a short period.

FUT vs. FUE: What is the difference?

FUT and FUE both move healthy hair from the back of your scalp to thinning areas, but they differ in how the grafts are collected, how the donor area heals, and the kind of scar they leave.

How grafts are collected A thin strip of scalp is removed and divided into grafts Follicles are removed one by one with a micro-punch
Donor scar Thin linear scar Tiny dot-like marks
Donor area shaving Usually a narrow area or none, depending on the plan Usually a larger area is trimmed or shaved
Graft numbers in one session Often suited to larger sessions Depends on donor density and session length
Recovery at the donor site Stitches, removed after a short period No stitches
Best suited to Patients who keep their hair longer and need many grafts Patients who like short hair or want to avoid a line scar

Who is a good candidate for FUT?

FUT works best for patients with good donor density and a flexible scalp who need a large number of grafts and usually keep their hair at a medium or longer length.

FUT may suit you if you:

  • Have male pattern baldness or significant thinning and need a high graft count
  • Have a donor area with good density and enough scalp flexibility for a strip
  • Usually keep your hair at a medium or longer length
  • Accept a thin linear scar in exchange for a large session
  • Are in good general health and have realistic expectations
  • Prefer to plan a transplant in Bangladesh instead of travelling abroad

FUT may not be the best fit if you:

  • Often wear a very short or shaved haircut
  • Have a very tight scalp with little give
  • Tend to form thick or raised scars
  • Have limited donor hair, an active scalp infection, or an uncontrolled medical condition

FUT procedure at HairTreat: step by step

Each step is planned by your doctor and adjusted to your scalp, donor hair, and the look you want.

  1. Step 1

    Consultation and scalp analysis

    The doctor examines your scalp, donor density, and scalp laxity, reviews your medical history, and discusses your goals.

  2. Step 2

    Hairline design and graft planning

    The hairline is drawn and agreed with you. The doctor estimates how many grafts you need and whether FUT is suitable.

  3. Step 3

    Donor strip removal

    Under local anesthesia, a narrow strip is taken from the donor area, and the wound is closed with fine sutures.

  4. Step 4

    Graft preparation

    The team divides the strip into individual follicular units under magnification and keeps them protected until placement.

  5. Step 5

    Channel creation and implantation

    Small channels are made at the natural angle and direction, and the grafts are placed to build a natural pattern.

  6. Step 6

    Aftercare and follow-up

    You receive washing instructions, medicine guidance, a plan for suture removal, and follow-up visits to check healing and growth.

Recovery and results timeline

Everyone heals at their own pace, so treat this as a general guide, not a promise.

Timeframe What to expect
First 1 to 3 days Mild swelling, soreness at the donor line, and small scabs on the recipient area. Avoid rubbing, scratching, or pressure on the back of the head.
Days 7 to 14 Scabs fall away, and the sutures are removed as advised by your doctor. Redness settles for most patients.
Weeks 2 to 4 Transplanted hairs shed. This is normal, and the follicles remain under the skin.
Months 3 to 4 New hair starts to appear, often fine at first.
Months 6 to 9 Hair thickens, and the density becomes more visible. The donor scar usually softens.
Months 12 to 18 Final density and texture are clear.

Possible risks and how we reduce them

Every surgical procedure carries some risk. With FUT, the main points to understand are:

  • A visible linear scar: The scar is usually thin, but it can widen in some people. Careful closure and a conservative strip width help limit this.
  • Numbness or tightness: Some patients feel temporary numbness or tightness around the donor line, which generally improves with time.
  • Infection or delayed healing: These are uncommon, and proper wound care and follow-up reduce the chance.
  • Shock loss: Some existing hairs near the donor or recipient area may shed temporarily after surgery.
  • Uneven growth or lower density: This is more likely when expectations or graft numbers are not planned realistically.
