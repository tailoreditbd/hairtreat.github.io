FAQ about hair transplant

Hair follicles taken from a stable donor area are generally resistant to the original pattern of baldness. However, existing non-transplanted hair may continue to thin with age.

A suitable candidate usually has permanent or stable hair loss, sufficient donor density, realistic expectations, and no uncontrolled scalp or medical condition.

The procedure is normally performed under local anesthesia, which helps minimize pain. Mild swelling, tenderness, or discomfort may occur during the first few days.

The required graft number depends on the size of the bald area, desired density, donor capacity, and hair characteristics. A scalp assessment is needed for an accurate estimate.

Temporary shedding may occur after the procedure. New growth often begins within three to four months, while noticeable improvement develops gradually over six to twelve months.

Many patients return to desk-based work within a few days. However, heavy exercise, direct sunlight, dust, and activities that cause excessive sweating should be avoided during early recovery.

The cost varies according to the technique, graft requirement, treatment area, and complexity of the procedure. HairTreat provides a personalized quotation after consultation and scalp assessment.
